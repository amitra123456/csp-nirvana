# Kirsten's Short Options Strategy Rules & Operational Creed

**Source**: `RULES` folder in the Google Drive archive (`IMG_4286`–`IMG_4289`), authored by **`kirstencumbiaparty` (Kirsten)** in `#education` under *"Best-Practice Guide on Short Options"* (dated 11/11/24).

---

## 1. High Probability, Larger Capital (The Market-Maker Posture)

* ~70% of retail options expire worthless. By selling options (CSPs and Covered Calls), you automatically place yourself on the opposite side of retail, operating similarly to a market maker.
* The trade-off is capital commitment:
  * Selling puts requires having the cash to purchase 100 shares at the strike.
  * Selling calls requires owning 100 shares outright.

---

## 2. Position Sizing & Zero Margin Rule

* **Plan for Deep ITM Assignment**: On any strike you sell, **ALWAYS** plan for assignment—and not only assignment, but deep ITM assignment. Ensure you can sustain that move and always plan for the worst-case scenario.
* This ensures you never risk more than is wise for your portfolio, and means your assignment can become a mid-term swing and exit for profit vs. being forced to cut at a deep loss because you are over-leveraged.
* **NEVER Use Margin to Sell Puts**: While accounts can be margin-enabled, **NEVER use margin to sell puts**. Leverage can multiply drawdowns and severely damage a portfolio. Every contract is strictly $100\% \text{ cash-secured} = \text{Strike} \times 100$.

---

## 3. Delta Judged by Chart Structure & S/R

* **No Rigid Black-Scholes Delta**: Delta is assessed by the chart—evaluating proximity to short-term, mid-term, and long-term support.
* **Closer ATM**: The closer to major structural support and the higher your willingness to be assigned, the closer ATM you go.
* **Farther OTM**: If favoring a lower-risk swing without assignment, select a level where price is realistically unlikely to breach before expiration, balancing as far OTM as possible while still collecting a premium that justifies tying up collateral.

---

## 4. Expiration Window: 4 to 6 Weeks Optimal

* **Core Window**: To capture larger, lower-risk premium ATM while benefiting from rapid theta decay, sticking to **4–6 weeks (~28 to 42 DTE, sweet spot: ~35 DTE)** is optimal.
* **Secondary Aggressive Style**: Lower delta, 1–3 week expirations when seeking quick, small premium wins.

---

## 5. Covered Calls (CCs)

* Selling calls against long shares after parabolic runs is an excellent way to capture premium.
* Approach CCs more conservatively than CSPs because broad equities have an upward drift.
* Never sell calls at a strike you aren't prepared to have your shares called away at.
* **Ironclad Rule**: *"I never close CCs for a loss, only roll for more premium or accept assignment."*

---

## 6. Assignment Management & Rolling

* Assignment is not a failure—it is a planned acquisition at a discounted cost basis.
* Strikes can be rolled indefinitely to capture incremental premium and delay assignment:
  $$\text{Effective Cost Basis} = \text{Strike} - \sum \text{Credits Collected}$$
* Every roll must collect an **additional net credit**.

---

## 7. Systematic Exits

* **Profit Taking**: Close early at **50% to 75% profit** (median: **60%–70%**).
* Set GTC limit buyback orders at 50% and 60% profit immediately upon entry.
